## The paper

Shen, Yuan, Yan and Ma, *MCST: An Adaptive Tracking Algorithm for High-Speed and Highly Maneuverable Targets Based on Bidirectional LSTM Network*, IEEE Transactions on Aerospace and Electronic Systems 61(2), 2025. Classical trackers such as interacting-multiple-model filters depend on a bank of motion models. When a target maneuvers outside that bank, the error grows. MCST learns the predict–update cycle of a Kalman-type filter directly from data.

## How it works

- **Sampling.** The state is a diagonal Gaussian. A few samples are drawn with the reparameterization trick, so uncertainty flows through the network.
- **Maneuver-compensation unit (MCU).** When the motion model is wrong, the residual between measurement and estimate concentrates at low frequencies. The MCU takes the FFT of the residual sequence, gates each frequency with a learned weight, and transforms back to correct the state.
- **Predictor.** A two-layer Bi-LSTM (64 units per layer) with attention over time steps and over samples outputs a mean and a log-variance.
- **Updater.** An MLP fuses prediction and measurement with a learned gain that plays the role of the Kalman gain, and also estimates measurement noise. Training minimizes MSE plus Gaussian negative log-likelihood.

## My reproduction

I reimplemented the model in PyTorch, including the maneuver-compensation unit and the Bi-LSTM predictor, and reproduced its adaptive tracking of high-speed, maneuvering targets. This was the main project of both courses.
