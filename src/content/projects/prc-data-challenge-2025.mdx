## The challenge

The 2025 PRC Data Challenge asked participants to estimate the fuel burned by commercial flights over given time intervals. The training data are intervals labeled with `fuel_kg`, each tied to a flight with its aircraft type, origin and destination, and a trajectory sampled in altitude, ground speed, vertical rate and track.

## What I built

A reproducible feature pipeline, about 2,600 lines of Python. It turns raw challenge data into one feature row per interval:

- **Trajectory features.** For each interval: duration, number of samples, mean and change in altitude, mean ground speed and vertical rate, distance flown, and mean heading change.
- **Aircraft specifications.** For each ICAO type code, a merged table of mass limits (MTOW, MLW, OEW), speed limits, ceiling, wing geometry, drag polar terms, engine bypass ratio, thrust and cruise SFC. The values come from OpenAP. Gaps are filled from the FAST AEROBASE database, mapped to ICAO codes by rule (for example A320neo → A20N, 777-300ER → B77W).
- **Weather.** Hourly ERA5 reanalysis from the Open-Meteo archive for every airport in the dataset. It's attached at origin, destination and an estimated mid-route point (great-circle midpoint, then nearest airport), with pressure at flight altitude from the barometric formula. Downloads are resumable, with retry and backoff.

Exploratory scripts check the target distribution, interval durations and fuel–duration relationships before modeling.
