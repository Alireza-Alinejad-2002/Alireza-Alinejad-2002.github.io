## Brief

The mission comes from the RAeS International Light Aircraft Design Competition 2024/25 RFP. It calls for a piloted, fixed-wing amphibian with six to eight seats for global use, certifiable to CS-23 with the water-load special conditions. At ISA + 20 °C it must fly a 750 km stage at 65 m/s or faster, hold for 30 minutes, and take off from water within 1,200 m in waves up to 0.6 m. We designed to that brief in the Aircraft Design 1 course; the project was not submitted to the competition.

## Configuration

We chose a conventional flying boat. It has a stepped hull with Dornier-style sponsons rather than wingtip or retractable floats, a high cantilever wing with no sweep and a 0.77 taper ratio, and single-slotted flaps. The two wing-mounted tractor propellers keep the engines out of spray, and differential thrust steers on water. The tail is cruciform, and the tricycle landing gear retracts into the hull and wing for land operations. Wing, empennage and nacelles are composite, and the hull is credited with a smaller weight saving because of saltwater exposure.

## Sizing toolchain

The team wrote its own MATLAB sizing code. It works through Roskam Class I methods:
- a technology-line regression of take-off weight against empty weight from comparable amphibians;
- mission fuel fractions with Breguet range and endurance for every segment, including a VFR reserve with an aborted landing;
- Roskam's analytical sensitivities;
- a matching diagram with CS-23 seaplane climb gradients and take-off, landing, cruise and turn constraints.

The sizing run gives an 11,490 lb take-off weight with aluminium structure. The Class I composite estimate brings that down to **11,061 lb (5,017 kg)**, about 58% of the brief's mass cap.

My sections were the empennage and the payload–range diagram. For the tail, I compared conventional, cruciform and V-tail options, and sized the cruciform layout from Dornier Seastar volume coefficients: a 65 ft² horizontal tail and a 44 ft² vertical tail. A dorsal fin was considered and rejected.
